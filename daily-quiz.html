The Daily Quiz

By the Sunday Pencil Club Editorial Team · Last reviewed: October 2026

Ten new questions each day, chosen automatically from our whole trivia bank. A small, agreeable ritual to pair with your morning cup.

On this page

  1. How it works
  2. A morning ritual

How the Daily Quiz works

Each day, the Sunday Pencil Club draws ten questions from our whole trivia bank and arranges them into today's quiz. Tomorrow it will choose ten more. The set changes with the date all on its own — there is nothing to sign up for, nothing to download, and no account to keep. Come back whenever you like and a fresh ten will be waiting.

Click an answer and the quiz responds at once, marking the right choice in green and keeping your running score at the foot of the page. There is no clock and no penalty; a wrong guess simply teaches you the right answer, ready for next time.

Making it a morning ritual

Many of our readers tell us they enjoy pairing the Daily Quiz with their first cup of coffee — a small, pleasant way to greet the day with a little curiosity. It asks only a few minutes, it never scolds, and it leaves you a fact or two richer than you woke up. If you share your mornings with someone, read the questions aloud and compare guesses; a quiz is always merrier as a duet.

Tip: Keep a little notebook by your chair and jot down any answer that surprised you. Writing a fact down in your own hand is one of the oldest and friendliest ways to make it stick.

Where the questions come from

The Daily Quiz shares its bank with our five themed trivia rounds, so on any given day you might be asked about an American river, a garden flower, a famous first, or the plain arithmetic of everyday life. Every answer is a settled fact that we have checked against reliable references before adding it. As ever, if you believe one is wrong, a short note to our editors sets us straight, and we are grateful for it.

No scores to chase

We want to be clear about what the Daily Quiz is not. It is not a test, not a competition, and not a measure of anything at all. There is no high score to protect, no streak to lose, and no one keeping count but you. If you miss a day, nothing happens; if you get three wrong, nothing happens. The only point is the small, agreeable pleasure of being asked a good question and reaching for the answer.

The small joys of a daily habit

There is a quiet comfort in a ritual that returns each morning without fail. The kettle, the curtains, the first cup, the daily puzzle — these small, dependable pleasures give a shape to a day and a gentle reason to begin it. The Daily Quiz is offered in that spirit. It is not meant to fill your time or improve you; it is meant simply to greet you, the way a familiar face across the breakfast table does, with a cheerful question and no demands.

Because it changes on its own with the date, there is nothing to manage and nothing to remember. You need not keep a streak, tick a box, or sign anything. You simply arrive when you arrive, answer today's ten, and get on with your morning a fact or two the richer. Some readers keep it for the first coffee, others for the lull after lunch; there is no right time but the one that suits you.

A short history of the quiz

The word “quiz” has a delightfully murky past, with a popular (if unproven) tale that a Dublin theatre owner coined it on a bet in the 1700s. Whatever its origin, the quiz as we know it — a set of questions with answers — became a beloved fixture of newspapers, radio, and the local pub across the twentieth century. The weekly pub quiz, the radio quiz show, the magazine quiz at the back of the issue: all are branches of the same friendly tree, and all share the same simple appeal of a question well asked.

Our Daily Quiz is a small, private version of that long tradition — no host, no studio audience, no prize but the pleasure of knowing. It keeps the best part of the quiz, the question itself, and quietly drops the rest.

Ideas for your own quiz nights

If today's ten whet your appetite, a homemade quiz night is one of the warmest ways to spend an evening with family or friends. Gather a dozen questions across a few subjects, split the room into teams, and keep the scoring loose and good-humored. Mix easy questions with a hard one or two so that everyone, young and old, gets a moment to shine. Our five themed rounds make a ready-made supply if you would rather not write your own.

Why ten questions

Ten is a number chosen with care. It is enough to give the mind a proper little stretch and to touch several subjects, but few enough to finish comfortably with a single cup of coffee, without ever feeling like a chore. A quiz that runs too long becomes a test; one that runs too short barely warms you up. Ten sits in the happy middle — a complete, satisfying round that leaves you ready for the day rather than tired of the exercise.

It is also an easy number to share. Ten questions split neatly between two people at the breakfast table, five each, or run as a quick family round where everyone calls out a guess. Short enough to repeat, long enough to matter, ten questions has the pleasing shape of a thing well sized for daily life.

The questions we leave out

Part of making a good quiz is deciding what not to ask. We keep well away from questions that turn on fierce opinion rather than settled fact — nothing that would start a quarrel rather than a happy “of course!” We avoid the fleeting and the fashionable, too, the sort of question that is out of date within a season, in favor of facts that have been true for a long time and will stay true for a long time yet. A good quiz question should be answerable, checkable, and the same next year as this.

We also steer clear of the merely obscure. There is no sport in a question that no reasonable person could answer; it only makes the solver feel small, which is the opposite of what we are here for. A fine trivia question sits in a happy spot — not so easy that it is no fun, not so hard that it is no use, but right at the edge of what a curious, well-read person might just recall with a satisfying effort. Finding that spot is most of the craft, and it is why we write and check each question by hand rather than scraping them from anywhere.

A companion through the seasons

One of the quiet gifts of a daily habit is the way it keeps you company through the whole turning year. The Daily Quiz is there on a bright summer morning and on the darkest afternoon of winter, exactly the same, asking its cheerful ten regardless of the weather or the mood. For readers who live alone, or whose days have grown quieter than they once were, that small dependable presence can be a genuine comfort — a familiar visitor who never fails to call.

We think there is real value in that steadiness, offered with no strings at all. The quiz will never nag you for missing a day, never congratulate you for a streak, never ask for a thing in return. It simply arrives, asks its questions, and wishes you well — the same tomorrow, and the day after, and the day after that.

A gentle word on keeping the mind busy

Many people tell us they enjoy a daily quiz as a pleasant way to keep the mind lightly occupied, in the same unfussy spirit as a crossword or a good book. We make no claims beyond that simple enjoyment — a quiz is a pastime, not a treatment, and it should be taken purely for the pleasure it gives. If keeping active and curious matters to you, the finest sources on healthy living are organizations such as the U.S. National Institute on Aging, which offers plenty of sensible, free guidance worth reading.

Part of a larger table of pleasures

The Daily Quiz is only one dish at the Sunday Pencil Club's table. If today's ten leave you hungry for more, everything else here is just a click away and equally free. The mini crosswords give the same facts a different shape, asking you to spell out an answer rather than choose it. The themed trivia rounds let you linger in a subject you love — geography, nature, history — for a whole round at a time. And the brain teasers swap recall for reasoning when you fancy a slower, deeper sort of puzzle.

We mention all this because variety is its own small tonic. Switching between kinds of puzzle keeps the whole business fresh, so that no single pastime ever becomes a rut. Some mornings you will want the quick satisfaction of the quiz; other days, the long, absorbing quiet of a word search or the stubborn delight of a riddle. There is no wrong choice and no set menu — only a table laid out for you to wander, at whatever pace and in whatever order pleases you best.

However you enjoy it, take it gently and let the brain teasers tempt you when you fancy a longer think.
